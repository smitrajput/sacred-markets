Explain it in 90 seconds

Sacred is a testnet MVP on Arc Testnet (chain 5042002) with one market, cirBTC, settled in real USDC through a fixed Uniswap v4 pool with fee 3000, tick spacing 60 and no hooks. Liquidity providers deposit USDC into a vault and receive non-transferable shares priced at NAV.

A trader puts up 0.10 USDC and is financed 0.10 USDC for a 2x fixed trade. The vault first funds the full 0.20 USDC from LP idle cash. The engine buys the BTC through a narrow adapter, records acquisition, disclosed resale and pledge, and only then collects the trader's 0.10 USDC. The financing carries a fixed 10% annualized markup over 14 days, split 77% to LPs and 23% to the manager, of collected markup.

If coverage falls to 160% or below, a gas-only keeper can trigger a partial sale. It cannot choose the size or oversell. Withdrawals run first in, first out. By design, the rules for shares, NAV, the markup formula, repayment order and the return of all remaining pledged BTC on full repayment are onchain. The daily price fixture, the separate keeper process and the thin pool are operator or testnet matters. The system is not audited and not production-ready.

Architecture

MarginVault records LP shares, NAV and cash buckets. TradeEngine sizes and executes trades and coverage actions. V4SpotVenue is the adapter that routes purchases and sales into the existing Uniswap v4 pool with fixed permissions (contracts/src/V4SpotVenue.sol). DemoPriceGuard supplies the reference used for collateral valuation. It is an admin-controlled testnet fixture, not a production oracle.

Balances, debt and transaction receipts come from Arc. The API formats public reads; the browser wallet sends signed transactions directly to the chain. If the API is unavailable, it cannot seize or change onchain positions.

Money buckets

NAV is LP idle cash plus outstanding principal. Reserves, reserved claims, manager cash and uncollected markup sit in separate buckets, so ordinary deposits and withdrawals use LP assets, not segregated funds. Reserves become LP cash only when explicitly applied to a realized principal loss (contracts/src/MarginVault.sol, verified locally). Donations mint no shares and do not inflate NAV.

Who absorbs a final loss?

A coverage sale first pays down principal. On final closure, any principal still unpaid is the gross principal loss. The vault covers it in this order; only the uncovered remainder reduces LP NAV.

Replenishment: the manager’s 23% of collected markup restores primary to 0.4% of the financing ceiling, then tail to 0.6%. Only the remainder becomes locked manager cash. New financing waits if either reserve is below its target.

Trade open to repayment

A 2x fixed trade has equity E = 0.10 USDC, gross purchase C = 0.20 USDC and financed principal P = 0.10 USDC. Opening requires gross cash of at least 2P, net idle cash of at least 5% of NAV, principal plus commitments of at most 95% of NAV, an empty withdrawal queue, and the ceiling of 1 and per-account cap of 0.10 respected.

The markup is 10% annualized over 14 days, fixed at open: M = floor(P × 1000 × 1209600 ÷ (10000 × 31536000)). At P = 100000 atoms (0.10 USDC), M = 383 and debt = 100383 atoms. Collected in full, the markup splits LP 294 and manager 89 micro-USDC.

Before deposits, new financing or withdrawal cash reservations, a fresh impairment gate scans existing live positions. If conservative net collateral is below remaining principal, the losing position settles first. A stale feed blocks new cash reservations, but reserved claims and full repayment or release stay available. Early repayment earns no rebate. A position with zero principal and markup above zero stays live.

Worked balance example (educational, not a live claim)

A 1 USDC LP deposit with 0.01 USDC of treasury reserves. After open: idle 0.90, outstanding P 0.10, NAV 1, reserves 0.01. On full repayment of 100383 atoms, LP idle and NAV reach 1.000294 and the manager's locked cash is 0.000089 if targets are full. Any BTC upside belongs to the borrower, not the protocol. Essential expenses are 0 and there are no company distributions.

Policy-scale illustration (distinct from the micro demo): P 500, proceeds 480, gross loss 20. Reserves 4 + 6 and a separate optional backstop of 5 leave an LP loss of 5. The gross loss of 20 is still emitted, and the unpaid markup is written off.

Participant journeys

Stablecoin depositor (LP)

  1. Connect the wallet and switch to Arc Testnet (5042002).
  2. Approve the exact USDC amount (6 decimals).
  3. Deposit. The vault mints non-transferable shares at floor(amount × supply ÷ pre-deposit NAV). Initial minting is 1:1 in micro-USDC only when the vault is empty.
  4. Collected LP markup raises NAV and the value per share. It is not a fixed depositor APY. New deposits and financing wait while withdrawal requests are unfilled.
  5. Request withdrawal. Shares lock, with no cancellation, into one global FIFO queue: 64 active requests maximum, up to 16 oldest processed per pass, partial head first.
  6. Queued shares remain exposed to gains and losses until filled. On fill, shares burn and cash is reserved. The later claim pulls this fixed payout without debiting NAV again.

Who earns future markup? Uncollected markup is excluded from NAV. Whoever holds shares when markup is collected receives its NAV benefit, including recent depositors. Shares already redeemed have no claim on later collections. This keeps accounting simple but does not track earnings by deposit vintage.

Leverage trader

  1. Connect the wallet, switch network, and approve the exact E = 0.10 USDC.
  2. Open the 2x fixed trade: gross C = 0.20 USDC, financed P = 0.10 USDC. All gates must pass.
  3. The vault funds all of C from LP idle; the engine buys BTC through V4SpotVenue.
  4. Acquisition, the disclosed resale and the BTC pledge are recorded in that order. The trader's E is collected only now. Any failure undoes everything.
  5. Monitor coverage V/D. The trigger is 160% or below; partial restoration targets 190% or above.
  6. Repay principal and markup. Partial repayment keeps the pledge. Full repayment returns all remaining pledged BTC to the original trader, whoever pays and even with a stale feed.

Liquidation (coverage) keeper

  1. Poll sequentially every 10 to 15 seconds.
  2. Read BTC atoms, the reference price and remaining debt D = P + M.
  3. Compute V = floor(BTC atoms × priceMicroUSDC ÷ 1e8).
  4. Check eligibility: coverage at or below 160%, maturity reached (after 14 days), or a pending queue action.
  5. Simulate the single candidate action. If simulation fails, send nothing.
  6. Send one transaction with maxFee at least 20 gwei and above the live base fee. Wait for the receipt, refresh state, and repeat on the next poll. A manual wallet button is the permissionless fallback.

Coverage keeper

The keeper is a gas-only external operator. It has no oracle, treasury or guardian power, and the engine chooses the sale size, so it cannot oversell. Nothing automatic is promised: each action needs a caller.

Partial-sale formula and worked illustration

Target sale amount g to restore coverage to R = 1.9 with friction f = 0.03: g = (R × D − V) ÷ (R × (1 − f) − 1), with the input rounded up. The engine checks post-sale coverage against the captured reference; its explicit rounding tolerance is ceil(price ÷ 1e8) + 1 micro-USDC. A caller’s minimum output cannot change the chosen quantity. If the intrinsic plan would leave principal below 0.01 USDC, it closes fully. If unexpectedly better partial execution instead creates a dust debt, the engine sells the remainder in the same atomic transaction; failure of that second leg rolls everything back. If the partial sale clears all debt, remaining BTC is returned unsold.

Illustration, not executed live yet: 195 BTC atoms at an 80k USDC reference give V = 0.156 USDC. With P = 0.10 USDC and M = 0.000383 USDC, coverage is 155.40%. Selling 52 atoms at a minimum of 0.040352 USDC leaves 143 atoms, V = 0.1144 USDC, debt 0.060031 USDC, and coverage 190.56%.

Guarantees, operators and testnet limits

The three columns are kept separate so no operator duty or testnet shortcut is read as an onchain guarantee.

Onchain rules (deployed and checked)

  • Shares are non-transferable. NAV = LP idle + outstanding principal.
  • Donations mint no shares and do not raise NAV.
  • Markup fixed at open; no late fees, rollover or trader profit share.
  • Collected markup split 77% LP, 23% manager, with cumulative floor rounding.
  • Repayment order: principal, markup, owner surplus.
  • Full repayment returns all remaining pledged BTC to the original trader.
  • Swap minimums derive from the reference with a 300 bps friction allowance; integer rounding is explicit. The configuration cap is 460 bps.
  • Guardian can pause new deposits and origination only. No upgrades, no arbitrary rescue.

Operator responsibilities

  • Refresh the DemoPriceGuard fixture at least daily and before each rehearsal.
  • For a price-drop demo, move the real pool first, then update the fixture explicitly.
  • Run the keeper as a separate process with its own gas wallet; keep its keys in ignored secret files inside the Studio sandbox, never in the frontend or read API.
  • Keep deployer, trader and keeper test wallets on testnet only.
  • Show the keeper as unavailable unless a real heartbeat exists.
  • Do not loosen safety limits to capture video.

Testnet simplifications

  • Arc Testnet only; one cirBTC market.
  • The fixture is admin-controlled. Pool spot is for execution diagnostics, not collateral pricing.
  • The pool is thin; quote checks are required, and the fixture may need refreshing between demos.
  • Maturity is tested with time warp.
  • Seed liquidity is small: 9.999976 USDC plus 0.0001 cirBTC.
  • Not audited; not production-ready.

Technical questions

1. Where does settlement run, and what is deployed?

Answer. Settlement runs on Arc Testnet (5042002). MarginVault, TradeEngine, DemoPriceGuard and a new engine-bound V4SpotVenue are deployed. The separate smoke adapter is retained for pool maintenance. Their runtime code matches the reviewed build outside verified immutable values.

Rationale. We verified the deployed v4 code on the selected chain and executed real swaps before building financing around it.

2. Why one asset and one chain?

Answer. One cirBTC market on Arc Testnet avoids integration risk. Bridging is off the critical path and not implemented.

Rationale. Scope stays within what can be verified. There is no cross-chain work, ENS, governance token or production oracle.

3. Why does the vault fund the full 0.20 USDC before buying BTC?

Answer. The engine buys BTC, records acquisition, executes the disclosed resale and holds the BTC pledge. The trader's 0.10 USDC is collected only after acquisition, and any failure undoes everything atomically.

Rationale. This preserves the sale-based financing design and keeps the purchase in one atomic sequence.

4. How is LP NAV defined, and can tokens sent to the vault inflate it?

Answer. NAV = LP idle cash + outstanding principal. Reserves, claims, manager cash and uncollected markup are excluded. Donations mint no shares and do not raise NAV.

Rationale. Separate buckets prevent routine use of reserves as LP liquidity; the settlement waterfall explicitly applies reserves to principal losses.

5. What happens when the price feed is stale or a position is underwater?

Answer. A fresh impairment gate runs before any cash reservation. If conservative net collateral is below remaining principal, the losing position settles first. A stale feed blocks new cash reservations, while reserved claims and full repayment or release stay available.

Rationale. DemoPriceGuard is a testnet fixture, not a production oracle, and pool spot only diagnoses execution.

6. Who decides when and how much a keeper can sell?

Answer. Anyone, including the gas-only keeper, can trigger an eligible coverage or maturity action. The contract checks eligibility and computes the sale quantity; the caller cannot select a larger sale. The keeper has no oracle, treasury or guardian power. Manual wallet execution is the fallback.

Rationale. The design avoids claiming a continuous guarantee that the hosting model cannot provide.

7. Why a fixed 10% annualized markup, and who receives it?

Answer. 10% annualized over 14 days, fixed at open, with no growing balance, late fees, rollover or trader profit share; early rebate is 0. At P = 100000 atoms, M = 383 and debt = 100383. Collected markup splits 77% LP and 23% manager.

Rationale. The trader gets a predictable cost, and LPs get a clear split of collected markup only.

8. Why doesn't the keeper run on Vercel?

Answer. The Vercel read API is serverless and does not run a persistent keeper. The keeper runs as a separate operator process, and the UI shows it unavailable unless a real heartbeat exists.

Rationale. This avoids an illusory continuous keeper and keeps signing keys out of the hosted app.

Design rationale

  • One asset and one chain avoid integration risk.
  • A real v4 adapter uses an existing protocol with fixed permissions.
  • The gross purchase order preserves the sale-based financing design.
  • A fixed markup gives predictable cost.
  • Cash bucket separation keeps ordinary LP liquidity distinct from reserves and unpaid markup.
  • FIFO gives liquidity priority.
  • Bounded scans suit a 32-position pilot.
  • An independent fixture is an honest testnet compromise, not production-ready.
  • A serverless read API is a simple host and avoids an illusory continuous keeper.
  • A non-upgradeable, limited surface.
  • Bridge and complex oracles are deferred.

Frontend, API and operations

Approved design · implementation in progress

This section describes the approved build; evidence above records what is complete. The vault and financing engine passed 138 independent tests. Review fixes cover caller-controlled sale sizing, stale/impaired origination, small-equity rounding and reentrancy. All four contracts are deployed and independently checked. Opening, full repayment and BTC release succeeded live; coverage-sale and withdrawal rehearsal are continuing.

Frontend

React 18, Vite, TypeScript, wagmi and viem, within the existing Sacred brand. Tabs are Earn, Trade and Activity, with an Arc Testnet badge. Each action follows: connect, switch, exact approval, action, confirmed receipt, authoritative refresh. Amounts use bigint or decimal strings: USDC at 6 decimals (shown to 6 places for small debt and split), BTC at 8, and native USDC at 18 with the same balance.

Quote boundary: the actual DEX output must also meet initial 190% coverage. At tiny trade sizes, rounding to whole BTC atoms can make an otherwise acceptable output floor insufficient; the interface must explain the rejection before submission.

Read API

Node/TS with viem, read-only. Endpoints: health, config, vault, positions and activity, using bounded log pages from the deploy block with caching. No database, no private keys, no signing endpoint. API health was tested locally in 8 cases. Vercel hosting for the frontend and API is configured through Studio; deployment is pending (server/handlers/health.ts).

Repository and operations

The repository is public, with small commits on main. The Studio origin is tracked through the share project and its history. A separate operator process runs with the gas-wallet key held in an ignored secret file inside Studio. Vercel receives no signing key. The user's browser wallet signs the user's own actions. Dedicated deployer, trader and keeper test wallets are used only on testnet, never mainnet.
